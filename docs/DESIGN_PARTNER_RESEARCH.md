## Interview request draft (not sent)

We are building PolicyLens, a toolkit connecting policies, internal controls,
AI-prepared investigation drafts and verifiable evidence. Could we speak with
the person who prepares evidence for banking partners or auditors? We would like
to understand the current workflow before proposing a pilot. A 20-minute call
needs no customer data or confidential case documents.

## Interview guide

1. When a banking partner or auditor asks for evidence, what do you send and how
   long does it take? Ask for a recent anonymized example rather than agreement
   with the product idea.
2. Who assembles the pack, which systems are involved, and what is missing?
3. How do policy versions, control approvals, alert dispositions and case notes
   get connected? What work is repeated?
4. What is the current monitoring/case engine and its documented export/API format?
5. Are AI-prepared drafts used? How do you record the model, evidence and human review?
6. What information may leave your environment? What review and agreement are
   required before a synthetic-data pilot or real-data integration?
7. Which one transaction source should the first adapter support? Request a public
   schema or generated sample, not a credential or real customer export.
8. What measurable outcome would justify a pilot: preparation time, omissions,
   reproducibility or review throughput? What would make you decline?

## Evidence ledger template

Record interview date, participant role, consent to retain notes, current tools,
an anonymized observed workflow, time taken, repeated work, desired export,
constraints, proposed metric and explicit pilot interest. Separate direct
statements from our inference. Keep any personal contact details outside Git.

Exit gates remain unfulfilled: five interviews, two or three recruited partners,
and at least three teams independently identifying evidence assembly as a real
pain. Public web research cannot satisfy those gates or determine the partner's
transaction adapter choice.
